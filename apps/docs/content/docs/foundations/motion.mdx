A component does not choose a duration. It chooses a role, and the role carries a duration, an
easing, and where relevant an offset and a scale, in whichever of the three motion modes is set.

<MotionRoles />

## Three modes, not a switch

`subtle` is the default and is what an interface should feel like. `expressive` is the same
vocabulary with more travel, for a product that wants it. `reduced` is what a reader who has asked
their system for less motion gets: durations under a ceiling, no travel, no scale, and nothing that
moves for its own sake.

Reduced is a mode in the token layer rather than a media query in each component, which is why it
cannot be forgotten in one component and remembered in another.

## The caps

Every role's duration is capped, and the cap is tested: `@hipuku/motion` holds each value inside
its ceiling, and a contract story checks that every animating component runs its role's duration in
every mode, that staggered delays stay inside the stagger maximum, and that reduced is both under
its ceiling and still.

That is the part a design system can actually guarantee. A component cannot promise to feel right;
it can promise never to take 600ms to answer a click, and never to ignore a reduced-motion setting.
