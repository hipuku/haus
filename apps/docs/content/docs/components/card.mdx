## Anatomy

One element, whichever one `as` or `asChild` supplies. It sits on `surface-raised` with
`radius-surface`, clips its own content, and takes `space-inset-lg` while `padding` is on. Turn
`padding` off for a card whose content runs to its edge, such as one that opens with an image.

A card has no header, body or footer parts. The content is the caller's, and a card that owned the
layout inside it would be a template rather than a surface.

## Variants

`variant` is elevation here, which is a visual weight rather than a meaning, so it keeps the name
`variant` (decision 0005).

| `variant` | Edge | When |
| --------- | ---- | ---- |
| `default` | `border-default` | A bordered surface. The one to reach for |
| `elevated` | `border-subtle`, with `elevation-floating` | Raised off the page, for a card that overlaps or floats |
| `outlined` | `border-strong` at `border-width-thick` | A selected or highlighted card, where the edge has to read at a glance |

## Owning the element

Two props, for two different problems, and decision 0022 is the rule behind both: a component owns
its box and never its element.

`as` is for the cases where the right element is a plain intrinsic one and the reason is the
document outline. A card is often a list item, an article or an aside, and a `div` in those places
is a hole in the outline that no styling fixes. The union is closed at five elements.

`asChild` covers what `as` cannot reach: a card that is a router link. `as="a"` would render
haus's own anchor, and the point of that case is that the framework's `Link` is the thing which
must render. The child supplies the element, Card supplies the box, and the child keeps its own
children, which is the opposite of IconButton.

The two are mutually exclusive in the types, because the child supplies the element and `as` names
one. Both class names are kept, Card's first and the child's after it, and which declaration wins
a collision is the ordinary cascade. The order inside the attribute decides nothing.

What does decide it is that Card's rules are in the `haus.components` layer. The cascade compares
layers before specificity, and an unlayered rule outranks a layered one, so a caller's own rule
wins wherever it is declared and however unspecific it is. Specificity only settles collisions
inside the same layer.

## Keyboard and screen reader

A card is a surface and takes no focus of its own. Anything focusable inside it keeps its own
place in the tab order.

A card rendered as a link or a button, through `asChild`, becomes the control, and the stylesheet
picks that up from the element rather than from a prop. Focus is then an `outline` in
`border-focus`, held off the edge by `focus-outline-offset` so the card's own backdrop shows
through as a gap. That is the same reading as the system's double ring and for the same reason
(decision 0001): the band is the accent at full strength, because alpha is what made the
treatments it replaced fall below the 3:1 WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 asks of a focus indicator. Under
`forced-colors`, where an outline is all that survives, it takes `focus-outline-forced`, which is
the system's own `Highlight` rather than haus's accent.

The outline is declared transparent at rest, so it transitions in rather than appearing, and the
card does not shift by its own outline width when focus lands.

`as="li"` is what keeps a list of cards a list for a screen reader.

## Motion

Only an interactive card moves. Hover takes the `border-control` edge and lifts the card by a
quarter of `motion-popover-enter-offset`; the colour runs on the highlight role and the lift on
the layout role, so the two scales stay in step with the rest of the system. Pressing scales the
card by `motion-press-scale` on the press role.

The lift is therefore a property of the motion mode: subtle moves it a little on an ease,
expressive moves it further on a spring that settles, and reduced changes the edge and nothing
else.

An elevated card also raises from `elevation-floating` to `elevation-overlay` on hover.

## Decisions

- **`variant` keeps its name here** (decision 0005). It is elevation, which is visual weight, and
  `tone` is reserved for meaning.
- **`as` is a closed union, not a polymorphic generic.** The generic kind costs a page of
  conditional types and makes every error message about the component unreadable, in exchange for
  type-safety on props nobody passes to a Card. What a caller needs is the right element in the
  document outline, not for `href` to typecheck on an `article`.
- **`as` and `asChild` are both here** (decision 0022), because widening `CardElement` to every
  element name would still not render a framework's `Link`.
- **The union prop type exports its halves** (decision 0023), so a consumer can write a function
  that takes one shape of Card.
- **`ref` is typed `HTMLElement`.** The union of elements makes React's own ref type an
  intersection that nothing satisfies, and the caller is the one choosing the element.
- **An interactive card has hover, focus and pressed states at all.** A card rendered as a link
  said nothing on either, so the one card in the system a pointer could use gave no feedback.
