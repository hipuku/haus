The primitive several other things are built on: haus's own `Select` panel is one, and so are the product menus a native popup could never theme.

`triggerRef` is required, and that is the point. vault's version leaves focus to the caller and none of its six call sites does it, so Escape drops focus onto `<body>` and the next Tab starts at the top of the document. WCAG 2.4.3, invisible to axe.

Not a focus trap. A modal is modal and the page behind it is inert; a popover sits over live content, so tabbing off the end closes it. There is no collision detection either: that needs a positioning engine, and this package declares `@hipuku/tokens` and the two React peers and nothing else.

## Anatomy

One element. The panel sits on `surface-overlay` with a `border-default` edge, `elevation-floating`
and `z-dropdown`, and takes `radius-control` rather than the overlay radius: a popover is anchored
to a control and reads as an extension of it, as Select's panel does, where a Modal floats free of
any control and keeps the overlay radius.

The panel is `border-box`, so one sized to its trigger is the trigger's width. On `content-box` the
padding and border were added on top, and Select's panel overhung its field by that much on one
side.

The caller owns the positioning context. The wrapper holding the trigger and the panel needs
`position: relative`, because there is no way for a child to establish that for its own parent.

## Placement, alignment and width

`placement` puts the panel below or above the trigger. `align` lines it up with the trigger's
`start` or `end` edge, or stretches it to the trigger's width. Both are logical, so the panel pins
to the side the text starts on and flips with the writing direction.

`width` is `auto` for a panel that takes its content's width, which is what a small action menu
wants, `trigger` for a panel at least as wide as the anchor, which is what a select-like menu
wants, or one of four fixed steps for a panel holding a form. The fixed steps read the
`popover-width` roles, so a brand can restate them.

`trigger` is a floor rather than a match: the panel starts at the trigger's width and grows to its
content from there, up to `popover-width-xl`, so a long option is readable instead of wrapped. It
is the trigger's width exactly only while the content fits inside it.

`align="stretch"` does not override that. Outside `portal` it sets both insets and nothing else, so
`width="trigger"`'s `max-content` still grows the panel; it is `portal` that measures the trigger
and sets the width outright. A fixed step keeps its own width under `stretch` either way. For a
panel that must not exceed its trigger, a fixed step is the setting that holds.

`portal` renders the panel into `document.body` and positions it from the trigger's rect instead of
against the caller's positioned ancestor. It is off by default and stays the caller's decision:
the absolute path needs no measuring and is right whenever nothing between the trigger and the page
clips, and it is wrong the moment something does, such as a Modal body that scrolls, where
`placement` cannot help because flipping to `top` clips at the other edge instead (`haus#68`). The
consumer knows whether it is inside a scrolling container and the panel cannot, which is the same
division `placement` already makes.

While portalled, the trigger's rect is measured and recomputed on scroll and resize, because both
move the trigger under a fixed panel, and the trigger's own direction is read with it, because
`start` and `end` are the trigger's edges and a fixed panel is placed in viewport coordinates.

## Keyboard and screen reader

Escape closes the panel and returns focus to the trigger. The return is the half every hand-rolled
popover forgets: Escape that only sets `open` to false leaves focus on an element that has just
left the document, and the browser's fallback is the body, so the next Tab starts at the top of the
page (WCAG 2.4.3). Focus is only restored when it was actually inside the panel, because a popover
dismissed by clicking elsewhere should not steal focus back from wherever the pointer deliberately
took the user.

Escape is handled in the capture phase and stops propagating, so a popover inside a dialog closes
the popover and not the dialog behind it. Nesting is the normal case rather than the exotic one.

Tabbing out of the last control in the panel closes it. This is deliberately not a focus trap, which
is what Modal has: a modal's page is inert and Tab has nowhere else to go, where a popover sits over
live content, and trapping focus in a small menu the user can leave by looking away is worse than
closing.

Outside dismissal listens on `pointerdown` rather than `click`. A click fires after the pointer is
released, so a drag that starts inside a menu and ends outside it closed the menu on release;
`pointerdown` also beats focus, which is what stops a second trigger's panel opening and closing in
one gesture. The trigger is not treated as outside, or its own click would toggle a panel this
listener had already closed, and the panel would open and shut on a single click.

`triggerRef` is required, and that is the point of the component. vault's version leaves focus to
the caller and none of its six call sites does it.

`role` is a closed union rather than a string. A popover with the wrong role is worse than one with
none: `menu` promises arrow-key navigation between `menuitem` children, and a panel that claims it
and holds a form is lying to every screen reader that believes it.

A name is required, as `aria-label` or as `aria-labelledby`, and the types take exactly one. A
panel a screen reader announces as nothing may as well have no role at all.

## Motion

The panel enters on the popover-enter role and leaves on popover-exit: a fade, a few pixels of
travel out of the trigger, and a slight scale. The direction of that travel is a custom property,
so a panel below the trigger comes down and one above comes up.

The tokens carry the mode. Subtle and expressive differ in duration, offset and scale, and reduced
has no travel and no scale, so it is a short fade.

The panel stays in the document while the exit runs, so it leaves on the exit role rather than
vanishing in a frame. While closing it is `inert`, so nothing in it takes a click or focus, and it
unmounts when the animation ends, or at once when there is no animation to wait for, which is also
what happens in a test environment with no CSS. Reopening during the exit goes straight back to the
enter animation.

## Decisions

- **`triggerRef` is required.** Focus returning to the trigger is the behaviour the component
  exists to guarantee, and it cannot be guaranteed from a prop a caller may omit.
- **Not a focus trap.** A popover sits over live content, so tabbing off its end closes it.
- **No collision detection.** Nothing flips and nothing shifts: the panel goes exactly where
  `placement` and `align` say. Detection needs a positioning engine measuring rects on scroll and
  resize, and this package declares `@hipuku/tokens` and the two React peers and nothing else, so
  adding one would make it a dependency of every consumer of every component. `placement` is the
  caller's answer to the same problem, one level up, where the layout is known.
- **`portal` is not that engine either.** It is the same placement in viewport coordinates rather
  than the ancestor's, and it is off by default.
- **`role` is typed, not a string.** A role is a promise about behaviour, and the wrong one is a
  promise the panel does not keep.
- **Outside dismissal is on `pointerdown`.** A click-based listener closes a menu on the release of
  a drag that began inside it.
